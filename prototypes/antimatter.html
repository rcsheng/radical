01 / Design — Antimatter

Inverse Design Console

Specify the material property you need. Antimatter screens billions of candidate compositions and hands the physical lab a short list worth actually synthesizing.

Why this screen exists Radical AI's own Antimatter page describes a four-stage loop — Predict, Optimize, Experiment, Feedback — combining atomistic simulation, active learning, and literature review to go from a target property to synthesizable candidates. This console is a plausible front end for that loop; the specific screens, fields, and copy are invented.

Goal

Find a gallium-nitride (GaN) alloy variant with a specific bandgap and thermal-conductivity target for a new power module — without waiting on a university collaboration or an 18-month internal R&D cycle.

Problem

The compositional search space for doped GaN alloys is far larger than her team can screen with in-house DFT simulation, and she has no way to prioritize which candidates are worth a physical synthesis run.

Explore vs. exploit Push this toward "explore" and Antimatter spends more of its shortlist on compositions further from anything it has data on — more likely to fail, but where genuine breakthroughs live. Radical AI's founders have described deliberately biasing toward this kind of search rather than only interpolating known chemistry, because that's where breakthrough materials tend to hide.

Feedback loop

This is the "compounding" part of what Radical AI calls the materials flywheel: every physical measurement doesn't just answer one question, it re-shapes Antimatter's confidence across the whole neighboring composition space — which is what makes the 100th experiment cheaper to choose well than the 1st.

The story this screen is solving

Realistic scenario

Priya's team needs a GaN variant that survives higher junction temperatures without giving up switching efficiency — the kind of incremental-sounding spec that's actually gated by a search space too large to hand-simulate. Historically this is a multi-year materials program: pick a handful of candidate dopants by intuition, DFT-simulate each one for weeks, synthesize the best guess, and hope. Antimatter compresses the "which candidates are even worth trying" decision from months of simulation to a single afternoon session, then hands off exactly two synthesis-ready candidates instead of twenty maybes — which is also what makes the Autonomous Lab's 25-alloys-a-day throughput (see console 02) actually useful instead of overwhelmed with noise.
