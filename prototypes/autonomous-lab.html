The candidates Antimatter shortlisted (console 01) become physical synthesis jobs. This is the shift-facing view of the robotic lab actually running them.

Why this screen exists Radical AI's Autonomous Lab page states the lab can "create and characterize 25+ alloys in one day," that each sample keeps "a complete digital trail: from synthesis parameters and annotations to characterization outputs," and that candidate selection aims to "maximize value per run, exploiting what looks most promising now while exploring new chemistries." This console makes those three claims operable as a dashboard; the exact UI is invented.

Why the full trail matters

"When every measurement follows the same method, comparisons across samples are meaningful in a way they aren't when manual measurements carry operator-to-operator variance" — from Radical AI's own account of standardizing characterization. The same logic extends upstream: capturing exact ramp rate and container lot, not just "annealed it," is what makes a result reproducible six months later.

The story this screen is solving

Realistic scenario

Three months after a promising alloy result, a customer asks Marcus to reproduce it at a slightly larger batch size — and the numbers don't quite match. In a conventional lab, this triggers a scramble through paper notebooks and Slack threads to reconstruct what actually happened. Here, Marcus opens the digital trail for the original sample in under a minute, sees the exact ramp rate, container lot, and dispense variance, and can tell within the hour whether the discrepancy is a real scale-up physics problem or a container-batch artifact. That's the difference between a lab that generates anecdotes and one that generates data Radical AI can actually train on.
