03 / Characterize — Microstructure Copilot

SEM Review & Annealing Call

Every synthesized sample (console 02) gets imaged. This is the screen a metallurgist uses to turn a microscopy image into a measurement — and the measurement into a decision.

Why this screen exists Radical AI's post "Scale, Speed, Precision" describes automating measurement: manual measurement took ~15 minutes per image and varied ±16% between operators; their automated segmentation model does it in ~1 second, trained on 10,000+ SEM images from nine months of autonomous lab operation. This console visualizes that workflow with an illustrative (non-photographic) dendrite diagram.

Goal

Set the correct annealing time for each batch — long enough to homogenize the microstructure, short enough not to waste station time other jobs are waiting on.

Problem

"Too little annealing time and the material isn't homogenized. Too much and you've wasted days or weeks" — and the SDAS reading that decision depends on used to vary by up to 16% depending who measured it.

Why consistency matters more than precision "When every measurement follows the same method, comparisons across samples are meaningful in a way they aren't when manual measurements carry operator-to-operator variance" — direct quote from Radical AI's post. A batch of 8 wildly different manual readings can't tell you whether your process is actually improving; a batch of 8 consistent automated readings can.

The SDAS reading feeds directly into how long this batch anneals.

Station-time reclaimed

~16–18 hours freed on this station alone — time that goes straight back into the queue Marcus manages in console 02. Multiply across every batch in a 25-alloy-a-day lab and the measurement-precision problem turns out to gate throughput, not just data quality.

The story this screen is solving

Realistic scenario

Before this tool existed, Elena's team defaulted every batch to a 24-hour anneal, because nobody trusted a manual SDAS reading enough to cut it shorter — the cost of guessing wrong (an under-annealed part failing downstream) was worse than the cost of wasting a day of station time. Once measurement stopped being the uncertain variable, the annealing schedule could finally respond to what the material actually needed instead of the worst case. It's a small-sounding fix — a segmentation model on a microscope image — that turns out to unlock station capacity across the entire lab.
